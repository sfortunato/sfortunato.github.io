# Linkedin Post Pass:

---

I gave Claude my resume. It decided I must be a man.

A few weeks ago I was using Claude to update my personal website. Here's the background I gave it: Epic, athenahealth, a startup I built from scratch and sold, a VP title at the company that acquired me. We had a nice chat, and then I linked to the old website so it could fill in some history. What came back was a polite suggestion that I'd probably linked to the wrong website.

"That domain suggests a woman's name," it told me. "This might not be your site."

It tried again a few lines later. Just to make sure I'd understood.

[screenshot]

I've been in tech long enough that I shouldn't have been surprised. But I was.

In a very specific, messed up way, it was almost a compliment. Claude was impressed enough to assume I had to be a man. Which is, of course, the problem. The whole problem, in miniature.

But this is the part that stuck with me. When a person underestimates you, you can react. You can let it register on your face, or say something, or decide it's not worth it. You can, over time, with enough small moments and enough patience and enough luck, maybe change someone's mind. With an AI, there is no feedback loop. No one to glare at. No possibility of impact. The model just moves on.

So I'm posting on the internet instead, which is not my preferred move.

My relationship with this industry has always been two things: deep love for the work, and the occasional reminder that the industry doesn't return it equally. That hasn't changed. It just showed up somewhere new.
